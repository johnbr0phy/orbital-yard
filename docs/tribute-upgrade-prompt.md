I want you to do a complete end-to-end pass on the Tribute War on your own, and make it the best-looking, smoothest and most watchable version it has ever been. The page is `armada-war-tribute-new.html`, and the scripts it loads next to it are `armada-battle-ai-new.js`, `armada-crew-new.js` and `armada-systems-new.js`. The Three.js port is `armada-war-three.html` plus `armada-three-*.js`, and it is regenerated by `scripts/build-three-port.py`. The tests live in `tests/tribute-new` and `tests/three`. I won't be around to answer questions. When something is unclear, make a reasonable call, write it down in a DECISIONS.md, and keep going.

First, who this is for, because it changes everything. It isn't for people who read our design docs. It's for someone scrolling X who sees a clip of Star Destroyers, Minbari cruisers and a Borg cube in the same sky and stops. It's for the person who opens the link on a four-year-old laptop or a mid-range Android phone and expects it to just run. It's for the fan who wants to lean back and watch a war the way you'd watch a match. If a viewer has to read the README to enjoy the first thirty seconds, the page has failed. The battle should explain itself.

**Where it stands today. Read this before you change anything.**
- It's one self-contained HTML file of about 920 KB, rendering with raw WebGL2 (`antialias:true`, `high-performance`). There is one forge worker, and the default battle is 600 ships with tactical reinforcement waves staged outside the perimeter.
- There are 23 fleets. Distant hulls fall back to three instanced representative meshes per class. The close, hero, selected, damaged and cloaked ships render individually. Planets and moons are real geometry with parallax.
- In the headless profile of a 600-ship muster, one simulated second cost about 5.55 s of CPU. That's now about 3.06 s, and prediction plus contact solving still dominate. A software-rendered Chromium run of 1,142 ships gets about 9 to 10 FPS during arrivals. Nobody has benchmarked it on a real GPU. PERFORMANCE-NEW.md, DISTANT-HULLS-NEW.md and THREE-PORT.md all say plainly that no 60 FPS claim is made.
- The only post-processing is a basic bloom: no HDR target, no tone mapping, no colour grading and no post AA.
- There is no audio at all.
- The camera director offers Action, All ships, Top down, Hero, Follow, Crew and Take control. Crew portraits are live Canvas renders capped at 10 Hz, and the HUD updates at 12.5 Hz.
- The UI is a war menu (Random / Curate), a score line, toasts, a ship card and the flight HUD. You can't control time, there are no replays, there's no kill feed and there's no end-of-battle story.
- Past bugs worth respecting: the debris "jerk" came from single-tick positional depenetration (DEBRIS-JERK-HANDOFF.md). Any new physics or interpolation work has to track `|Δpos − v·dt|` per tick, not just velocity deltas.

Read every `*-NEW.md` doc, then run the existing test suites and the headless battle, and write down the baseline numbers before you touch a line. Don't optimise anything you haven't measured.

**Performance: make it run, then make it fly.**

Measure first. Build a `?perf=1` overlay that shows frame time split into sim, AI, collision, forge, render submit and GPU time, using `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` where it exists. Also show draw calls, triangles, live ships, projectiles, particles, debris and JS heap. Write a deterministic benchmark script: fixed seed, fixed fleets, 200, 600 and 1,200 ships, a scripted camera path, and a report of p50, p95 and p99 frame times. Every performance claim in this pass has to come from that script, with before and after numbers in a PERFORMANCE.md table. Say clearly which numbers came from software-rendered headless Chromium and which from a real GPU, and never present the first as the second.

Targets. State your assumptions in DECISIONS.md.
- 60 FPS at 600 ships on a 2020-class integrated-GPU laptop, and 30 FPS or better at 200 ships on a mid-range phone.
- First ship on screen in under 2 seconds. The battle should be fully playable in under 5.
- No long-task over 50 ms during arrivals, and a flat heap across five back-to-back wars.

Simulation:
- Use a fixed-timestep sim (for example 30 Hz) decoupled from rendering, with interpolated transforms at display rate. Keep it deterministic so seeds still replay identically.
- Move hot ship state to structure-of-arrays typed arrays. Kill per-frame allocations: there are dozens of `new Float32Array` sites, so audit every one. Pool projectiles, particles, beams and debris.
- Use a uniform spatial hash for broad-phase collision, sensor queries and target acquisition, and stop scanning the whole fleet.
- Give the AI a level of detail. Ships far from the camera and from any enemy think less often in staggered buckets. Anything on screen or selected keeps full rate. Prediction and contact solving get the same bucketing.
- Look into moving the sim into a worker and passing transforms back in transferable buffers. GitHub Pages can't set COOP/COEP headers, so if you want SharedArrayBuffer, either use a service-worker shim or don't. Decide, document why, and don't break the no-build, open-the-file ethos.

Forge and loading:
- Run a pool of forge workers (`hardwareConcurrency - 1`, capped) instead of one.
- Build the muster progressively: heroes and capitals first, so the arrival cinematic starts while fighters are still being forged.
- Cache generated meshes in IndexedDB keyed by seed and forge version, so the second visit is instant.

Rendering:
- Give every class an instanced path with per-instance transform, paint and damage attributes, and one draw per class per level of detail.
- Build a 3 to 4 step LOD chain per class, with a dithered crossfade and hysteresis so nothing pops.
- Frustum-cull hulls, and occlusion-cull them behind planets.
- Batch weapons and beams as instanced quads, and move particles onto the GPU.
- Sort by program and state, and put per-frame uniforms in UBOs.
- Use dynamic resolution scaling to hold the frame target, and cap devicePixelRatio by quality tier.
- Offer quality presets (Low / Medium / High / Ultra), auto-detected on first run from a two-second probe, and remember the choice.
- Pause cleanly on hidden tabs, which already works, and throttle when the page is idle behind a menu.

**Graphics: make every frame a poster.**

The look should be "every fleet we grew up with, lit by the same sun." Each franchise stays recognisable in silhouette from across the battlefield, but they all share one coherent, cinematic lighting and colour language, so the frame doesn't look like 23 different games pasted together. Before you change a shader, build a visual style sheet as a page in `design/tribute-new`: a palette per star system, a paint and material sheet for each of the 23 fleets, weapon colour and shape signatures side by side, explosion scale tiers (fighter, frigate, capital, First One), and a "do not do this" section. On that list: blown-out bloom, rainbow soup, lens flares on everything, identical grey explosions, and hulls that read as flat plastic.

Pipeline:
- Render the scene into an RGBA16F HDR target. Tone-map it with ACES or AgX, and add auto-exposure with a slow adaptation so a capital ship's death flash blooms and then settles.
- Replace the bloom with a proper dual-Kawase or mip-chain bloom driven by emissive energy, not raw brightness.
- Grade colour per star system. Add a subtle vignette and film grain, both off in reduced-motion or low tiers. Chromatic aberration stays off by default.
- Use MSAA on the hull pass plus FXAA or SMAA on the composite, and make thin beams and fighter silhouettes stay crisp.

Lighting and materials:
- The key light comes from the system's actual star, at the same direction and colour as the planets use.
- Add a cool fill from the nebula or planet bounce, and a rim light that separates dark hulls (Shadows, Borg, Imperial) from black space.
- Use a lightweight PBR model: metalness and roughness per fleet paint, and per-vertex ambient occlusion baked in the forge, which is free at runtime.
- Add procedural panel-line and greeble normal detail that fades in with screen size.
- Light up windows and running lights, and put engine glow on each fleet's real drive colour.
- Let big bodies shadow small ones. Planets eclipse ships. A shadow map for the hero or selected capital lets fighters pass through a Star Destroyer's shadow.

Space itself:
- Build a procedural nebula skybox per system, and a starfield with real colour temperatures and a faint galactic band.
- Add near-field dust motes that streak with camera speed so motion reads, and soft god rays from the star when it's partly occluded.
- Use aerial perspective inside nebula systems so distance reads as depth.

Weapons and impacts:
- Keep the existing per-fleet signatures (bolts, beams, pulses, kinetic bursts, plasma, ion charge-ups, First One arcs) and give each an HDR core, a coloured sheath, a muzzle flash and an impact response.
- Shields where the lore has them: Trek shield bubbles that ripple at the hit point, Minbari and Shadow hits that flare the hull itself, Borg regeneration shimmer.
- Leave glowing scorch decals where shots land, fading over time.
- Make ion charges build visibly, with gathering light and a warning volume, so viewers learn to dread them.

Destruction is the money shot, so treat it that way:
- Fighters pop with a flash, a shockwave ring and a handful of glowing shards.
- Frigates break into a few pieces with secondary explosions.
- Capitals get a staged 5 to 10 second death: internal blasts marching down the hull, venting atmosphere, the ship breaking in two along a seeded fracture line, lights flickering out section by section, and a final core flash that lights every nearby hull.
- First Ones should die like the end of an age.
- Debris glows at its fracture edges and cools from orange to dark over time. It keeps the dead ship's momentum, as it does now, and never jerks.
- Damaged ships show it: fires, trailing smoke or plasma, flickering lights, and hull breaches that grow.

Arrivals per franchise:
- Imperial and Rebel ships drop out of hyperspace with a stretch-and-snap.
- EarthForce and Minbari come through a jump point vortex, and Starfleet, Klingon and Romulan with a warp flash.
- The Borg arrive through a transwarp conduit. Tyranids emerge from a warp rift, and the Tesla fleet on a rocket burn.
- These are original interpretations: no copied assets, audio or logos.

**UI and playback: make it entertaining to watch.**

Treat this like broadcasting a sport. Most people will watch, not fly, so the watching experience is the product.

The first five seconds:
- The page opens straight into a live cinematic arrival of a random matchup behind a minimal title and two buttons, "Watch this war" and "Choose fleets." No settings wall.
- Pick the opening matchup for spectacle: two iconic fleets with at least one capital each.

The fleet picker:
- Show a card per fleet with a slowly turning hero ship rendered live, an original emblem in the fleet colours (no trademarked logos), one line of flavour, and a matchup odds bar computed from fleet strength.
- Let the viewer call the winner before the battle starts, and tell them at the end whether they were right.

The in-battle HUD, broadcast-style and minimal, fading when the mouse is still:
- Top bar: a tug-of-war strength meter with both fleet colours, ship counts, and icons for surviving capitals and heroes.
- Side panel: a small momentum graph over time.
- Kill feed, top right: "Home One destroyed by Executor", with capitals and heroes emphasised and fighters grouped ("14 TIE fighters lost").
- Event ticker for notable moments: ion charge fired, hero duel, reinforcements arriving, capital breaking up, cloak revealed.
- Hovering a ship shows its label. There's an optional radar or minimap.
- Show the pilot "thoughts" that already exist as short captions on the ship being followed. They're the soul of the page.

The director becomes event-driven:
- Keep Action, All ships, Top down, Hero, Follow, Crew and Take control. Add a Broadcast mode that follows shot grammar: an establishing wide shot, then a tension build on an ion charge or two capitals closing, then a cut to the climax, then a reaction shot of the survivor or a hero's captain portrait.
- Score events by importance (capital kill > hero duel > ion strike > squadron wipe > dogfight), and have the director cut to the highest-scoring event in view or about to happen. Use a hold-time floor so it never ping-pongs.
- Big kills trigger slow motion: the sim time scale eases to 0.25x for about 2 seconds, then eases back. Keep it deterministic.
- Add a picture-in-picture inset when two major events overlap.

Time controls:
- Pause, 0.25x, 0.5x, 1x, 2x and 4x, from buttons and number keys.
- Instant replay: a ring buffer of compact sim snapshots covering the last 15 to 20 seconds lets you replay the last big moment from a different angle. Press R, or tap the replay chip that appears after a capital kill.
- After the battle, a scrubbable timeline with event markers.

The end of the battle is its own scene:
- A victory card with the winning fleet, the battle's duration, losses on each side and an MVP ship with its captain portrait and seeded service record.
- A short auto-written "story of the battle" built from the event log. It's factual, three to five lines, and never lies about what happened.
- A highlight reel of the top five moments, replayed from the snapshot buffer.
- A share button that saves a PNG card and, where MediaRecorder exists, a 15 to 30 second WebM of the highlights, ready to post on X.

Mobile and accessibility:
- A proper phone layout with thumb-reachable controls. Pinch to zoom, drag to orbit, tap to follow.
- Colourblind-safe faction markers: shape plus colour, not colour alone.
- Reduced motion turns off shake, grain and slow motion. There's a keyboard shortcut sheet on `?`.
- Every panel closes, and closing never breaks follow.

**Audio: there is none. Build it.**

Write every sound yourself with the Web Audio API. Use no franchise sound effects or music.
- Each fleet gets a weapon voice: laser zaps, phaser hums, kinetic chatter, organic spits, ion charge whine rising to a crack.
- Explosions scale with size, with low-pass filtering and delay by distance so a far capital death arrives as a late, low rumble.
- Engine drones fade in when you follow a ship.
- Add a UI click palette.
- An adaptive original score: a sparse ambient bed that rises in intensity with combat density and drops to near-silence for slow motion, then hits a single stinger on a capital kill.
- It's muted until the first user gesture, per autoplay rules. Add master, music and SFX sliders, and keep a hard cap on simultaneous voices so audio never costs frames.

**Constraints:**
- Keep the no-build, single-page ethos. It still has to work by opening the file or serving it from GitHub Pages.
- Keep seeds deterministic. The same seed has to produce the same war, and the tests must keep passing, with new tests for everything you add: the event scoring, the replay buffer round-trip, time-scale determinism, LOD selection, pooled-object lifetimes and the audio voice cap.
- Decide whether the WebGL2 page or the Three.js port is the canonical one going forward. Document why in DECISIONS.md, and if both survive, keep `build-three-port.py` producing a working port.
- These are fan tributes: procedural, low-poly interpretations with original emblems, sounds and music. Nothing ripped, and no claims of canon.

**Verification loops. Don't skip them.**
- After every batch of changes, run the full test suites and the benchmark script, and compare against the baseline. A graphics change that costs more than 1 ms at 600 ships on the reference tier needs a written justification or a quality-tier gate.
- Use Playwright with the preinstalled Chromium (`/opt/pw-browsers`). Capture screenshots at fixed timestamps for three seeded matchups, and keep a before and after gallery in `design/tribute-new/review`.
- Look at every screenshot yourself. Is each fleet readable in silhouette? Are dark hulls separated from space? Does the bloom respect the tone map? Do explosions differ by tier? Is the HUD legible at phone width?
- Watch a full battle start to finish in Broadcast mode and log every bad cut, dead stretch, unreadable moment or hitch, then fix them and watch it again.
- Check debris and replay smoothness with the `|Δpos − v·dt|` harness.
- Keep a log of what worked and what didn't, so you stop repeating mistakes.
- Ask one final question: would someone who has never seen this page watch a whole war without touching anything, then hit replay and send it to a friend?
