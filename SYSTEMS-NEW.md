# Tribute New physical star systems

Every new battle generates one of eight seeded star-system arrangements. Planets, moons and stars now occupy fixed world coordinates. Their meshes use the same perspective and retained depth buffer as ships: camera translation produces parallax, approaching increases apparent size, and a world hides ships behind it. Stars remain a distant starfield. Physical body placement is separated so planet and moon surfaces do not overlap.

Surfaces use multi-scale procedural terrain, continents, clouds, polar ice, gas bands, subdued rock and ice patterns, lava and solar granulation. Normals rotate in world space while surface coordinates remain attached to the body. Lighting has a dark hemisphere, water highlights and an atmospheric rim. Additional close-range detail fades in according to screen footprint. Sphere meshes are built once per war at 5,120 triangles each; complete generated celestial geometry including solar halos and rings stays below 80,000 triangles in the seed sweep. Old buffers are disposed at regeneration.

Opaque worlds render before ships with their depth intact. Transparent rings and solar halos sort by distance and render after opaque ships and wrecks. Worlds are no longer anchored to the camera or forced behind every combatant. The camera far plane follows travel distance.

C toggles cruise while piloting, or use the Cruise touch button. Cruise raises travel speed and slows near the nearest surface; braking, reducing throttle or firing cancels it. The HUD shows the nearest named world's surface distance. Player motion uses swept sphere contacts so cruise cannot tunnel through a planet. Ordinary ships use inexpensive axis checks before resolving surface penetration. Free flight also stops outside solid bodies. The vertical flight clamp is removed. These are orbital bodies with solid surface bounds, not a terrain-landing or ground exploration system.

Validation: 200-seed determinism, all eight layouts, separated physical surfaces, geometry budget, actual battle setup regeneration, unchanged world positions under camera movement, growing angular size on approach, swept high-speed contact, tangent flight, interior recovery, and a real helm flight to a generated world's surface. Helm/crew, watch, rendering, effects, weapons and hull-damage checks passed. Actual WebGL shader and world rendering were inspected in isolated distant and near-surface previews. Full-battle GPU performance was not benchmarked.
