# Tribute War polish pass: decisions

Calls I made without being able to ask, and why. Numbers here come from `scripts/bench-tribute.cjs` and the headless harness. PERFORMANCE.md has the full tables and says which numbers are software-rendered.

## Who it is for

The brief put the viewer first: someone who clicks a link from X and wants to watch. So wherever a choice traded detail for "it just runs and explains itself", I took the second. The page now opens straight into a live war behind a two-button title, and Broadcast is the default camera.

## Canonical page

**The WebGL2 page (`armada-war-tribute-new.html`) is canonical.** The Three.js port stays and is still generated by `scripts/build-three-port.py`.

- All simulation, UI and broadcast logic lives in the WebGL2 page. The port is a mechanical transform that replaces only the draw section of `frame()`. It inherits the new HUD, event log, director, time controls, replay ring, end card and audio. It does not inherit the new image pipeline, lighting or new effect sprites, because those are the draw section.
- The WebGL2 page works from `file://` and GitHub Pages with no module loader. The port needs an HTTP server (ES modules plus vendored three r160), so it can't honour "open the file" in every browser.
- Keeping the port costs almost nothing: the build script needed one pattern updated (`mat(frameVP)`), and the `tests/three` parity suite passes against the regenerated port.

## Image pipeline

- **The scene shaders are still display-referred.** Every hull, ring and dust shader was authored as display values. Rather than rewrite them, the composite decodes the RGBA16F buffer with a 2.2 power into linear light. Normal content round-trips unchanged. Stacked additive light (explosions, beam cores, engines, windows) keeps its excess above 1.0 instead of clipping, and only that excess feeds bloom.
- **The default tone curve is the shoulder of Khronos PBR Neutral with its toe removed. ACES is available with `?tonemap=aces`.** Both were tried on the same frame (seed 77, Executor orbit):
  - ACES fitted crushed the dark end (display 0.1 → 0.057) and compressed the hull mid-tones (the 0.5–0.9 band shrank from 0.40 to 0.31 of output range). Panel detail washed out and hulls read as flat plastic, which is on the do-not list.
  - Full PBR Neutral's toe crushed faint content: planet rings and the slate floor at 0.1 went to 0.02.
  - The shoulder-only curve is identity below 0.8 and rolls highlights off smoothly with film-like desaturation.
- **Auto-exposure only darkens.** Space is mostly black, so a symmetric auto-exposure would pump empty sky up to grey. Here the adapted geometric-mean luminance can only pull exposure down, to at most 0.32×. It reacts to a death flash in about 0.3 s and recovers over about 1.2–2.5 s.
- **Bloom is dual-filter** (4/5-tap down, 8-tap up). It is fed only by luminance above 1.5× white with a soft knee, and coarse levels are weighted at 0.62 so the glow stays tight. Early versions produced white blobs in capital death clusters (224 overlapping kind-11 flashes up to 576 px), so the threshold went up, the flash HDR gain went down, and the level count dropped by one per tier.
- **Grades run at half strength**, with contrast pivoting at 0.3 so black never crushes. A full-strength "Ember worlds" grade turned neutral grey hulls mauve.
- **MSAA on the hull pass plus FXAA on the composite.** MSAA uses a multisampled RGBA16F renderbuffer resolved by blit: 0, 2, 4 and 4 samples for Low, Medium, High and Ultra. FXAA always runs. The default framebuffer keeps `antialias:true`. The build script matches that exact line, and it's harmless because the only thing drawn to it is the composite triangle.
- **Chromatic aberration is not implemented** (the brief wanted it off by default). Grain and vignette are off in reduced-motion (grain) and absent in the Low tier (grain).
- **Fallbacks.** Without `EXT_color_buffer_float` the pipeline uses RGBA8 with no HDR but keeps grade and FXAA. If pipeline creation throws, the page falls back to the old direct-to-canvas path. `?post=0` forces that path for A/B checks.

## Light

- **The key light is the system's star**, the same direction the planets use (`starSystem.light`).
- **Hulls take only 10% of the star's colour.** Planets take none, so taking all of it would break "the same sun". A red star painted the whole Imperial fleet pink in testing.
- **Real star direction means some shots are back-lit or lit from below.** That is physically consistent with the planets. To keep form I strengthened the fill (0.16 → 0.26), added a cool rim (stronger on dark hulls: Shadows, Borg, Imperial grey) and kept the hemispheric sky term.
- **Planets eclipse ships.** The four worlds nearest the battle are analytic sphere occluders in the ship shader.
- **No shadow map.** A fighter-through-Star-Destroyer shadow map needs a second depth pass of the hero hull every frame. It's the most expensive item on the lighting list, so I didn't build it, and I'd gate it to Ultra if I did.
- **One dynamic explosion light.** The brightest recent death flash lights nearby hulls: warm for thermal deaths, cold for ion and reactor deaths.
- **Not done:** PBR metalness and roughness, baked vertex AO, and procedural normal detail. The ship shader keeps its existing gloss, specular and procedural paint system. The style sheet documents intended roughness and metalness, clearly marked as intention.

## Time, determinism and smoothness

- **The sim stays on its fixed 1/30 s step.** The war clock (pause, ¼, ½, 1, 2 and 4×, plus slow motion) only changes how many steps a wall second buys. `tests/tribute-new/timescale.test.cjs` shows a war run through pause, ¼×, 4×, slow motion, 2× and ½× matches the 1× war at every shared step count.
- **Rendering interpolates between steps.** It interpolates ship, round and debris transforms between the last two 30 Hz steps, then restores the true state after drawing. The existing 30-versus-120 FPS test still produces identical combat. Placement jumps over 500 m aren't interpolated.
- **Slow motion only triggers on big kills the camera can see**, and not in reduced-motion mode. It eases to 0.25× over 0.35 s, holds 1.7 s (2.4 s for a First One), then eases back.

## Simulation performance

- **Measured first.** The headless harness takes 3,118 ms of CPU per simulated second at 572 ships (matching the old 3.06 s note). Prediction's broad phase padded every query by the fastest speed on the field times a 5–16 s horizon, so the spatial index fell back to scanning every hull for every ship.
- **Replaced the prediction broad phase with a swept-box index**, plus numeric cell keys in the existing index. The candidate set is a guaranteed superset in the same order, so the chosen threat is identical. A recorded three-battle trace is byte-identical before and after, and the cost fell to 2,196 ms per simulated second (−30%).
- **Not moved to a worker.** The simulation shares objects with GL mesh handles, DOM updates and the replay ring. GitHub Pages can't send COOP/COEP, so SharedArrayBuffer would need a service-worker shim, and that breaks `file://`. Copying 1,200 transforms back each tick is feasible, but the refactor touches most of an 18,000-line script. I spent the time on measured hot paths instead.
- **No structure-of-arrays rewrite, and no camera-based AI level of detail.** A camera-dependent AI rate would make the war depend on where the viewer looks, which breaks "same seed, same war". A sim-only version (think less when far from any enemy) would save about 6% by the profile. I left it out because it changes combat.
- **Consequence, stated plainly:** at 572 ships the simulation still needs more CPU than a 60 FPS frame budget allows on this container's CPU. The frame caps sim work at 10 ms, so a 600-ship war plays slower than real time there. The quality tiers therefore choose the default battle size, and Ultra is the only tier that defaults to 600 a side.

## Forge and loading

- **A worker pool of `hardwareConcurrency − 1`, capped at 4.** The old per-worker "hopeless band" cache depended on which jobs a worker had seen, so hull seeds would have changed with core count. It is now a deterministic probe per (fleet, band). `forge-pool.test.cjs` checks one worker against four.
- **Results stream in small batches, capitals and heroes first.** The old single message carried the whole muster and made one long upload task.
- **`fractureMesh` and the fire-hull BVH were rewritten with byte-identical output.** Verified against the old functions on 40 random meshes. The single-thread forge for 572 ships went from 11.4 s to 7.1 s.
- **No IndexedDB mesh cache.** A 572-ship muster is 64 MB of typed arrays, which is too much to write into IndexedDB on every new seed. The pool and the faster forge addressed the actual cost.
- **No progressive battle start.** Muster placement (`openTheMuster`, `spreadMuster`, `spaceTheFleets`) needs every hull's size, so the war starts when the muster is complete. The live title screen covers forging on first visit.

## Broadcast

- **Event scoring:** First One kill 130 > capital breakup 96 ≈ capital kill 100 > hero kill 92 > hero duel 70 > ion strike 62 > squadron wipe 42 > dogfight 12. "About to happen" events (ion charge, capital or hero low on hull under fire) score highest just before they pay off.
- **Director:** establish → build → climax → reaction.
  - Each phase has a hold floor, and a global floor of 3.2 s.
  - An urgent event (score ≥ 90, 1.25× the current shot) may cut once the global floor has passed.
  - The same subject within 30 s is penalised.
  - It goes wide at most every 25 s.
  - A full Empire vs Rebels war had 45 cuts: a minimum hold of 3.06 s, no ping-pong, no cuts to dead ships, and all 7 capital and hero deaths on screen as they happened.
- **Automatic replays.** Borg vs Federation loses capitals in bursts, so only 4 of 12 were live on camera. Broadcast therefore auto-replays a big kill it missed ("let's see that again"), at most once every 18 s. Highlights keep the rest.
- **The story never asserts causation.** An early draft wrote "the tide swung … when X met Y". The turning-point line now reports the window and what happened in it.
- **Pilot captions use the older flavour lines.** `thoughtLine()` returned AI telemetry for every ship, so the flavour writing had become unreachable. The captions build on that writing by hiding the AI object from the text builder only.
- **Picture-in-picture is not built.** A second full scene render costs a frame, and it wasn't worth it on phones.
- **Replay is a snapshot ring, not re-simulation.** It records 10 Hz snapshots of transform, pose, liveness and hull for every ship, plus capped weapon segments, over 20 s. Flashes are logged with their start times. Dead hulls keep their GPU mesh for 24 s, or longer while a highlight clip pins it. Wrecks are shown by back-extrapolating their velocity. That is exact for drift but approximate for wrecks that collided during the window.
- **R now means replay.** Random war moved to G. N still opens the picker.

## Picker

- **Emblems are original and procedural**: a seeded frame and motif per fleet in its own colours, with no logos.
- **Odds come from a real headless round-robin**: all 253 pairings at 20 ships a side, fitted as Bradley–Terry ratings on an Elo scale by `scripts/fleet-ratings.cjs`. They are labelled "simulated odds".
- **No live turning hero ship per card.** Twenty-three forged hero meshes and viewports on a phone menu contradicts the performance targets. The live war behind the menu does that job.

## Audio

- **Everything is synthesized** in `armada-audio-new.js` with oscillators, noise and filters. There are no samples or franchise sounds.
- **It is silent until the first pointer or key press**, with a hard cap of 24 voices (12 on Low).
- **Sounds come from observing the simulation.** New beams and rounds each step are sampled (at most 10 per step) and deaths come from the event log, so audio can't affect the war.

## Targets and assumptions

- "600 ships" in the brief is read as the benchmark's total ship count: 300 a side, which makes 572 ships once musters are cut. The old default of 600 a side made 1,142 ships.
- The reference laptop is a 2020 integrated GPU (Intel Iris Xe / UHD 620 class) with a 4-core CPU. The phone is a mid-range 2021 Android (Adreno 6xx class).
- No real GPU was available in this environment. Every browser frame time here comes from SwiftShader and is labelled that way. `scripts/bench-tribute.cjs --headed` on real hardware produces the numbers this document can't.
